Why now

  • Detroit's downtown decade is compounding: Michigan Central reopened (2024), the 2024 NFL Draft drew a record ~775,000 people downtown, and summer weekends routinely move 30–50k through the core.
  • The day-club category is proven and growing — Miami, Vegas, Scottsdale monetize a pool deck at $150–300/guest/day. The Midwest has demand and zero supply.
  • Land is the arbitrage: the single biggest cost that makes coastal day clubs expensive is nearly free here by comparison.

Why us

  • We've already built the pool. Working container-pool prototype — lined, filtered, decked (photo on the Concept tab). This is not a rendering company.
  • The venue is a fabrication project — welding, electrical, wraps, rigging — and fabrication is our home field. Self-build saves ~$180k vs turnkey retail on pools/deck/bars alone.
  • Engineering-led compliance: we treat EGLE plan review as an engineering deliverable, not paperwork — stamped drawings, turnover calcs, VGB compliance from day one.

The concept

Miami's Strawberry Moon (The Goodtime Hotel, South Beach) proved the formula: a pool, a deck, cabanas, a DJ, and food that photographs well — sold as a day-club ticket, not a hotel stay. Detroit has no equivalent, three months of pent-up summer demand, and something Miami doesn't: cheap flat land in the middle of the action. We bolt the venue together from shipping containers — which we already know how to build.

✅ We've already built the hard part

Our working prototype: 20-ft container pool (unit CSR 20-336), lined, filtered, with a cedar deck skirt. This is why the budget's "self-build" track exists — retail container pools run $35–80k installed; ours come out of the shop for a fraction of that.

⚠️ The one thing that can kill this: pool licensing. In Michigan a pool "used collectively by a number of individuals" is a public swimming pool under Act 368, Part 125 — it needs an EGLE construction permit (engineered plans, plan review) and an operating license with Detroit Health Department inspection before anyone swims. Container pools are not pre-approved designs, so engage EGLE's pool program and a pool engineer in month one, not month six. Budget and timeline for this are built into the Budget tab. Full checklist on the For Sale & Permits tab.

For the engineers — the build, quantified

💧 Aquatics

  • Each 40' pool: 8'×40'×~3.8' avg ≈ 9,100 gal; 3 pools ≈ 27,300 gal total
  • Commercial turnover target 2 hr → ~75 gpm filtration per pool, NSF-50 sand filters
  • Automated chem feed + ORP/pH controllers; dual VGB-compliant main drains per pool
  • Heat: ~130k BTU heat pump per pool (or NG boiler bank) to hold 82–84°F on Michigan June nights
  • Fill via hydrant permit/meter; sanitary-approved drain-down plan for teardown

🏗 Structural

  • Containers on engineered compacted-aggregate pads + concrete piers at corner castings
  • Water load ~76,000 lb per 40' pool — well inside container rail capacity when continuously supported; stamped calcs anyway
  • Deck: freestanding steel-framed modules, 100 psf assembly live load, 42" guardrails
  • Fabricated as bolt-together modules in the shop → 2-week site assembly, full winter teardown option

⚡ Electrical & code

  • ~200A/480V temp service (or paralleled gensets): pumps 3×2HP, heat, ~30kW sound/light
  • NEC Art. 680 throughout the wet zone; GFCI everything; bonded deck steel
  • Code path: EGLE pool plan review → BSEED structural/electrical → fire marshal (assembly/egress) → Health Dept operating license
  • ADA: ramp to deck level + fixed pool lift

Planning-level engineering, consistent with the prototype build — stamped design comes from the licensed pool engineer during EGLE plan review.

Downside case, plainly

Worst credible season (8 events survive weather/permits at 800 heads, $35 tickets): ~$21k net/event → ~$170k season — covers overhead and debt service on the equipment note but not payback. The response is programmatic (more dates, buyouts, corporate) rather than existential: the steel doesn't rot, the license persists, and season 2 starts with zero CAPEX. The unrecoverable scenario is failing EGLE licensing — which is why engineering leads the schedule.

Marketing plan

The product is the content: pools + skyline + a crowd that dresses for it. Strategy: manufacture scarcity (ticketed Saturdays, capped capacity), let the venue photograph itself, and ride Detroit's existing summer calendar instead of fighting it.

1 · Brand

Working name: MOONDOCK — full moon over the shipping docks; nods to Strawberry Moon without borrowing it. Alternates: The Slip · Dry Dock Social · Freighter Club · High Tide DET.

  • Look: blush pink + aqua + navy on raw corten steel; container wraps by local muralists (the shop's wrap team prints them in-house).
  • Voice: "South Beach called. We sent it to voicemail."

🏊 Community benefits package — offered, not extracted

  • Free community swim mornings — weekday mornings the pools sit idle; we open them, lifeguarded, to neighborhood families and day camps at no charge.
  • Learn-to-swim blocks with Detroit Parks & Rec / YMCA partners — swim access and water safety are real equity gaps; three heated pools can chip at that all season.
  • $1 per ticket → a neighborhood fund steered with the district's community organizations (~$19k/season at base case).
  • Detroit-first hiring and paid lifeguard certification for Detroit residents — a certified-lifeguard pipeline the city's own pools can hire from.

🏙 What the city gets

  • Vacant-land activation — exactly the stated goal of the city's land strategy: an underused lot becomes a maintained, insured, taxpaying venue with eyes on the street.
  • Tax base: sales tax on ~$1.4M of season revenue, payroll taxes on 70+ jobs, and a property that's worth more licensed than idle.
  • Spillover: 20k visits feed nearby restaurants, bars, and rideshare before and after every event.
  • A national story: "Detroit built a day club out of shipping containers" is earned media the city doesn't have to buy.

🤝 Programs we intend to work with

The honest civic pitch

Detroit doesn't need another rendering of a megaproject. It needs small, fast, visible wins on land that's doing nothing — built by people from here, staffed by people from here, generating receipts the first summer.

This is a two-container-crane, one-season experiment with a real balance sheet behind it. If it works, it scales to a second site and a longer season. If the city wants proof before promises: the prototype pool already exists, the budget is public on this page, and the community benefits are in the plan before the first permit application — not negotiated after.

What we need from the city is speed, not money: a named permitting contact, a realistic special-event/seasonal-venue path, and a fair hearing at BSEED.

📋 Permit & compliance path (Detroit / Michigan)

Critical path Pool licensing

  • EGLE public-pool construction permit — engineered plans to EGLE's pool program before building (program page). Container pools = custom design, allow 60–90 days review.
  • Operating license + Detroit Health Dept inspection before opening.
  • Design to code: turnover rates, main drains/VGB, depth markers, fencing, lifeguard plan.
  • Hire a pool engineer who has taken a novel design through EGLE. Non-negotiable.

City Land use & events

  • BSEED zoning review — outdoor commercial recreation; expect a conditional-use hearing in most B/M districts. Temporary-use permit for structures >120 sf.
  • Special-event application — 60 days minimum before each event (or permit the site as a seasonal venue once).
  • Health Dept temporary food licenses (~$250) for food vendors.
  • Noise: aim speakers inward, 10pm–11pm curfew posture, befriend the neighbors early.

State Liquor & safety

  • MLCC: nonprofit special licenses are the event hack, but a real season wants a licensed operator partner or a purchased Class C + outdoor service permit. Start MLCC paperwork 4–6 months out.
  • Liability + liquor liability insurance (~$2.5k/event inside the model), workers' comp for staff.
  • Fire marshal: assembly occupancy, egress, extinguishers; electrical inspection on temp power.
  • ADA route to deck level (ramp) + accessible restroom trailer.
Suggested sequence: tie up land with a lease-option → BSEED pre-application meeting + EGLE plan review kickoff (same week) → MLCC path chosen → winter shop fabrication → spring site work & inspections → licensed by mid-May → Memorial Day open.
