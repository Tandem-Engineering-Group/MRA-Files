Check rent control before you decide

Most Pacific Heights apartment buildings qualify

San Francisco rent control covers buildings of 2+ units with a certificate of occupancy on or before June 13, 1979 — which describes most of the Victorian and Edwardian stock in this neighborhood. Annual increases are capped (roughly 60% of CPI, historically 1–3%/yr).

Exempt: single-family homes and condos (for tenancies starting after 1996) and anything first built after June 1979. Those can be raised to any market rate at renewal — in a market that just moved 20%+ in a year, that difference can dwarf every amenity premium on this page.

Practical rule: between two similar units, a slightly pricier rent-controlled flat often beats a cheaper exempt one within two or three years. Verify a building's status with the SF Rent Board.

Layout moves, ranked by return

What actually moves rent in a 1900 Victorian duplex, in the order worth doing. Dollar impacts are monthly rent, from the factor benchmarks on the guide tab.

1 · Add a second bath to the upper flat
A 3BR/1BA is the classic Victorian handicap — a second bath is the single biggest unlock. Stack it over the existing wet wall (bath or kitchen below) to keep plumbing cost down. A powder room (half bath) captures most of the value if a full bath doesn't fit.
+$250–400
2 · In-unit laundry in both flats
A stacked washer/dryer fits in a hall closet or bath. Scarce in pre-war stock, cheap to add, and it widens the applicant pool as much as it raises rent.
+$100–150 each
3 · Give the lower flat exclusive yard access
The 3,510 sq ft lot means there's a rear yard. Deed it to the lower unit as a private garden — it flips the ground-floor discount into a premium and is pure paperwork plus a gate.
+$150–250
4 · Split the garage into two rented spots (if present)
Spots in the area rent for $375–450/mo à la carte. Offering one per unit — or renting them separately — monetizes space that adds nothing to livability.
+$375–450 each
5 · Kitchen/bath refresh over gut remodel
Renovated finish is worth +5–10%, but in a rent-controlled building you can't re-set rent later — so match spend to the rent tier you're targeting at initial lease, not resale taste.
+5–10%
6 · Keep bedroom count — don't merge rooms
An open-plan 2BR rents for less than a modest 3BR here. If the upper flat's 9-room layout allows a small 3rd bedroom or office-sized room, preserve it; families and roommate groups pay for the door.
protects rent
7 · Soundproof between the flats
Underlayment + ceiling insulation during any renovation. Doesn't raise asking rent directly, but it cuts turnover — and under rent control, keeping a good tenant at a well-set rent is the whole game.
cuts turnover
